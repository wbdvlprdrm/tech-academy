# Tech Academy landing page critique

Method: dual-agent (design review + detector/browser evidence)

## Overall Impression
The page has a coherent, authored story: practical technology learning connected to the founder’s call-center experience. The core opportunity is to align that story with a concrete, truthful offer. The biggest trust problem is that the page says visitors are joining a list and will receive updates, while submissions currently remain in the browser.

## What's Working

- The hero gives visitors a clear starting point: headline, supporting explanation, one primary CTA, and an illustration tied to the “English to tech” premise.
- The page says topics are being explored rather than presenting them as courses already available.
- The founder story and explicit no-employment-promise language provide useful context and realistic expectations.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] The form’s promise conflicts with what it does
The page invites people to join an interest list, asks permission to contact them, confirms “You’re on the list,” and says feedback will shape programs. But the form notice says responses stay local, and the adapter only logs them in the browser. A visitor can reasonably believe their details were received when they were not.

**Fix:** Decide whether submissions will reach a real destination. Make the CTA, consent, privacy note, and success state describe that same behavior. Until then, present the form honestly as a local prototype.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

### [P1] The primary audience and offer are still broad
The hero singles out English-speaking call-center professionals, then expands to anyone exploring tech. The form spans zero experience through advanced, while topics and program format remain exploratory. Visitors may not know whether this is for them or whether a course is available yet.

**Fix:** State who the first program is primarily for, what is confirmed about it, and what is still being explored. Keep secondary audiences secondary.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

### [P2] The page takes a long route to its main action
The learning path, founder story, audience grid, and expectation-setting sections repeat parts of the same “build skills and progress” message. That adds scrolling before the interest form without resolving open questions about the offer.

**Fix:** Give each section a distinct job, consolidate repeated reassurance, and bring the form closer once the audience and offer are clear.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable distill`

### [P2] Supporting text is too small for a long, information-dense page
Form labels and options are around 9–10px; topic descriptions and notes are similarly small. Detector findings reinforce the legibility concern: 49 `tiny-text`, 57 `undersized-ui-text`, and 43 `low-contrast` warnings.

**Fix:** Increase form and supporting-copy sizes and line height, then review contrast across light and dark sections, especially at mobile widths.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable typeset`

### [P2] Mobile behavior is not verified in a rendered viewport
The CSS stacks major sections and grids at narrow widths, which is a sound baseline. Without a browser capture, visual clipping and real-device legibility cannot be confirmed. The detector also flagged tight padding and tracking patterns.

**Fix:** Review a phone-width render after content changes, focusing on hero artwork, card text, form controls, and section-to-section spacing.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable adapt`

## Persona Red Flags

- An English-speaking call-center professional may connect with the headline but still wonder whether the program is primarily intended for people like them.
- A visitor looking for a course may interpret topic cards as available programs despite the exploratory caveat.
- A visitor who submits personal details may expect follow-up, while the local-only notice says the response is not sent anywhere.
- Someone with prior experience may not know what the broad beginner-to-advanced range means for their learning path.

## Minor Observations

- The display typography and navy/lime palette are consistent, but tight negative letter spacing and numerous all-caps kickers create a dense, highly art-directed feel.
- The hero illustration is distinctive, though its code-card motif communicates “technology” more clearly than the learning experience.
- Mobile layouts stack cleanly in CSS; clipping and real-device legibility remain unverified.

## Questions to Consider

- What should a visitor expect to happen immediately after joining the interest list?
- Which learner should see themselves first in the hero?
- What details about the first program are confirmed today, and which are still open?
